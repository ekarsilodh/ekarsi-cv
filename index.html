Ekarsi Lodh

I build computational methods for genomic, epigenomic and clinical data, from DNA methylation clocks and polyploid GWAS to fine-tuned clinical language models.

I hold a BTech in Computer Science and Engineering and an MSc in Bioinformatics from the University of Birmingham (2026). I am currently a Research Associate in the Department of Cancer and Genomic Sciences and a Postgraduate Teaching Assistant, benchmarking fine-tuned large language models (InternistAI-7B, Gemma-2-9B) for clinical prediction on EHR data with QLoRA/PEFT.

I am first author of six peer-reviewed papers, in journals including the Journal of Computer-Aided Molecular Design, Journal of Molecular Graphics and Modelling and Computational Intelligence, spanning deep learning for protein–ligand binding, GPU-accelerated sequence alignment and reinforcement learning for drug prioritisation. I build reproducible Python and R pipelines for genomics, epigenomics and healthcare data on HPC (BlueBEAR), and I teach and mentor computational research.

Research areas

My work sits where biology meets machine learning, often bringing both to the same question.

  • Cancer epigenomics

    Epigenetic clocks and age acceleration in early-onset breast cancer, and gene-expression models for PAM50 subtype prediction.

  • Clinical language models

    Benchmarking zero-shot and QLoRA fine-tuned LLMs (InternistAI-7B, Gemma-2-9B) on longitudinal EHR data for mortality, readmission and next-diagnosis prediction, with fairness evaluation.

  • Signalling and drug repurposing

    Boolean and Bayesian network models of faulty signalling pathways, molecular docking, and offline reinforcement learning for drug prioritisation.

  • Protein–ligand binding

    Deep learning architectures (ResNet-LSTM, CNN-GRU) for predicting binding affinity.

  • Polyploid GWAS

    Association pipelines for canopy architecture and yield in autotetraploid potato, with population structure and BLUE-based correction.

  • Sequence algorithms

    GPU-accelerated global alignment that scales from genomic to proteomic data.

Research

Mar 2026 – present

Research Associate

Department of Cancer and Genomic Sciences, College of Medicine and Health, University of Birmingham, UK

Supervisor: Dr Mamunur Rashid

  • Benchmarking zero-shot and fine-tuned LLMs (InternistAI-7B, Gemma-2-9B) for clinical decision support on three longitudinal EHR datasets: MIMIC-IV (364,627 patients), Synthea (~575,000 synthetic patients) and a Delphi-2M synthetic UK Biobank cohort. Tasks cover mortality, 14-day readmission and next-diagnosis prediction, with ethnicity- and sex-stratified fairness evaluation.
  • Fine-tuning with QLoRA (4-bit NF4; full- vs last-layer adapters) on GPU HPC, and comparing full-history vs recent-visit prompting against XGBoost, DoctorAI, Delphi-2M and Longformer baselines.
  • Fine-tuning raised InternistAI's MIMIC-IV mortality AUROC from 0.678 (zero-shot) to 0.946, above XGBoost (0.939). Fine-tuned LLMs achieved the highest AUROC on all three readmission datasets (MIMIC-IV: 0.766 vs 0.736 for XGBoost).

Nov 2025 – Aug 2026

MSc thesis project

School of Biosciences, University of Birmingham, UK

Supervisor: Dr Archana Sharma-Oates

  • Investigated epigenetic age acceleration and earlier breast cancer onset in UK-resident South Asian women using blood-derived DNA methylation data across multiple cohorts.
  • Integrated five epigenetic clocks (Horvath, Hannum, PhenoAge, GrimAge, DunedinPACE) with robust preprocessing, cell-composition adjustment and multi-clock triangulation to assess associations with breast cancer risk and age at diagnosis.
  • Built reproducible statistical and ML workflows in R and Python, with a focus on under-represented populations.

2026

Clinical mortality risk prediction using fine-tuned clinical LLMs

Machine Learning module project

  • Developed in-hospital mortality prediction models using structured EHR data inspired by MIMIC clinical datasets.
  • Fine-tuned Internist-7B using QLoRA (4-bit PEFT) for efficient clinical outcome prediction.
  • Fully fine-tuned BioClinicalBERT and benchmarked against XGBoost, LSTM and transformer-based models.
  • Evaluated with AUC-ROC, precision, recall and F1-score. GitHub release planned.

2025

Streamlit trio VCF analyser for structural variant interpretation

Interactive web app, based on the Genomics & NGS module project

  • Ingests VCF files from trio-based structural variant calling and summarises SV types, genomic locations and functional context.
  • Infers trio roles (child and parents) and sex from X-chromosome heterozygosity, and detects de novo mutations through Mendelian consistency checks.
Launch app Source code

Delly-VCF-Trio-Analyzer: command-line SV interpretation pipeline

Reproducible analysis workflow, Genomics & NGS module project

  • End-to-end pipeline for parsing, summarising and prioritising structural variants from Delly-generated trio VCF files, including QC, de novo calling, annotation, tiering of pathogenic variants and plotting.
  • Produces structured output tables, text reports and visualisations that slot into downstream clinical or research pipelines.
